A Spirit Dealer

This story is set in the world of Pale and Pact as written by Wildbow. Please go read those stories! They aren’t required reading, this is intended to be an introduction into the world of Practitioners and Spirits, but the original stories are way cooler. https://palewebserial.wordpress.com/

This street was like all the others: a long somewhat winding road lined with near identical family homes. Perfectly watered green lawns, white picket fences, the works. Almost all were kept in perfect condition, this was one wasn’t.

Thomas didn’t need to read the house number to know it was his destination. It reeked. Not that he could smell it with his nose, but it smelled all the same.

Goblins.

He stopped at the front gate. His cigarette still had a ways to go. He didn’t want to waste it. Plus, it gave him time to think.

When he met his client earlier, Mia, she had described what most would have called a rodent infestation. Dead things kept popping up, left on the doorstep, or rotting smells leading them to find a small mouse under a table, or a dead bird in the garage. Most were easy enough to explain, just happenstance. Mice and small birds were hardly out of the ordinary, they get stuck somewhere, starve, die. An innocent pest controller might say there was a stray cat living somewhere in their home and was bringing them in. His client hadn’t thought much of it initially. But it kept happening.

It was the coyote, impaled on their front fence that drove Mia to come to him. She’d found it when taking the kids to school one morning. There it was, its head stuck on one of those perfect white pickets, blood and brains strewn across their perfectly green grass.

It wasn’t there anymore. But the stains were. The white picket fence was soiled in brown and black flecks of dried blood. Mia had tried to scrub it clean, but it was done poorly. He couldn’t blame her, finding something like that would scare anyone.

She had gone to the police, of course, but they brushed it off as well. It must have jumped over and got stuck on the fence. No investigation, they didn’t even bother to ask her to see a counsellor despite her near hysterical state. They didn’t even comment on her children, who must’ve seen it too.

So she’d come to him.

The problem was, once he’d looked into it. It was more than dead birds. But they were hidden from innocent view. Neighbour’s pets had gone missing. Missing children. She’d had a husband before. Memories of the missing or deceased were fuzzy for those who weren’t aware, memories lost, or altered to suit their new reality, unable to confront what they’d seen.

From what he’d pieced together, it looked to be a case of goblins. Dirty, crude, and tricky little creatures. Anything involving lots of dead creatures being placed around aiming to scare of just stink up the place as markings of their territory was an obvious sign of them. The deaths were those who were unlucky enough to spot them.

But it didn’t all fit. Goblins were filled with base instincts, to fight, to frighten, or to fuck. They left carnage in their wake, they don’t pick and choose targets. Mia and her kids were fine, at least physically. Most of the neighbours as well, nothing amiss. No injuries. But they were tormenting Mia about something she couldn’t think of why, nor could he. He didn’t know her well enough to guess.

Not to mention this was a well-built suburb. Goblins rarely came this far into civilised areas. Crossing water, electricity, any flow of energy was hard for them. How they had gotten this far was another mystery.

And most crucially of all: there weren’t any giant dicks drawn in blood or faeces either. A favourite goblin pastime.

And yet. It stank of goblin. Something else was here as well.

A goblin queen.

He’d taken a long trip out of the city to see the house first hand. He didn’t even need to get out of his car to know she was lucky to have come to him. He sent her a message, telling her to take her three kids and go to a hotel for a few days while he sorted out the problem. He couldn’t have her around while he worked. She’d be drawn into this world. He didn’t want to be responsible for her.

He went on a short trip to a hardware store, by the time he’d returned Mia and her children were gone.

He looked at the house, his cigarette was almost done now. He took one last breath, letting it warm his lungs up. He put it out, and hefted the car battery and coils of lights held in his left hand. He tapped the pistol underneath his jacket making sure it was still there. He opened the gate and walked into the stench.

The path leading to the front door was short, but he went to the garage first. Goblins would stay away from a house, no plumbing, fewer lights, and out of sight. He set the car battery down and plugged in the set of Christmas lights, still coiled. The lights burst to life, all shades of green and red.

The colours of goblins. He mused, actually not the best choice now that he thought on it. But it would work.

He gave himself some slack as he approached the side entrance. The door was unlocked. He opened it carefully, eyeing the inside. It looked ordinary, gardening tools, packets of soil. The scent was strong here though.

He threw the lights inside first without fully uncoiling it. He stepped inside, through the entrance and into the centre of the lights.

Stepping inside, and the smell hit him again. Rotten meat mixed with shit, the copper taste of blood hit his tongue too. He wished he could have another cigarette to get the taste out of his mouth.

He saw it then. A small green creature, staring at him from behind a shelf. He was barely two feet tall, his head almost half his height. His face was stuck in a permanent smile… staples, Thomas saw, pinning his lips and cheeks in a forced grin. His teeth and been filed unevenly like chipped serrated knives.

“Is it just you then?” Thomas called out to the small thing.

The goblin’s fake smile dropped, hanging off it’s staples. Then widened even further. “No, of course not! But it is just you, isn’t it? You practitioners are always soooo lonely.” It teased, a sing-song-like cadence, contrasting with its absurdly deep and croaky voice, like you’d expect to come from a toad.

“I’m more than enough for you. Surely, you know of me?” He kept his hands still. The lights should be enough, but he readied himself to reach for his pistol at the first sign of movement.

“Hi, thanks for coming.” Thomas held out his hand for his hopefully new client to shake as he entered his office. The old man ignored him, just entered the office and moved to one of the seats. “Have a seat.” Thomas said, after the man had already sat down.

He sat down across from the old man while his cold grey eyes wandered over Thomas, lingering on his likely ill-fitting suit. It didn’t suit the area very well, but it was necessary. It was who he had declared himself to be. The more he embodied the professional, the more he would be allowed to be professional. Surely the old man knew this.

“You’re The Spirit Dealer are you?” The old man said with an air of fake nonchalance, looking around his office.

“Yes, sir.” Thomas said. “And you are the Lord of Anaheim. Thank you for coming, and thank you for hosting me in your Kingdom.” Thomas opened a draw and pulled out a piece of paper, folded carefully and wrapped in some pink yarn. “A spirit of the mountains. It’s killed thirty innocents by Maple Springs trail, I now offer it to you, as tribute for being allowed to start business here.”

Thomas put the paper on the desk between them, but kept his finger on it. Eyeing the Lord carefully, trying to appease him.

“This is my service, I find dangerous Others, bind them…” Thomas smiled then, he couldn’t help himself. “And offer them to the highest bidder.”

He spied the second goblin with well enough time. It dived at him face first. This one was larger, a whole three feet tall, but it’s body was much larger, obese as word is underkill for it. Thomas whipped his pistol out from its holster and slammed it against the goblins oversized forehead, driving it into the ground despite it likely weighing over eighty pounds.

It had crossed the barrier of lights. Stupid goblins. Barely worth anything either.

The fat goblin slammed back into the ground with a sickening squelch.

He could have ignored it, it wouldn’t have been strong enough to hurt him. But he had to send the message.

The smaller goblin grimaced and growled at him.

“You two have caused quite the stir. The owner of this property is innocent, you know the rules. Surrender and I’ll drop you off outside the city.”

The fat goblin howled with laughter at that rolling around on the ground slamming his little fists on the concrete.

His strength had come back awfully quickly…

Thomas kicked a loop of the Christmas lights over the goblin, lighting up his green boil covered skin with yellow, red, and green incandescent glow. His laughter stopped as he tangled himself up, he wasn’t able to even lift an arm to move the lights.

Two of them would be enough for what’s happened here. Anymore and he would be surprised that they hadn’t caused more chaos.

But the smell.

This place stank more than could be caused by two goblins. It only made sense if there was some Other keeping them under control.

“I ask again, surrender yourself, and I’ll let all of you live.” He looked around the garage, pointedly. He couldn’t see any more goblins, but they had to be there.

He heard snarls, laughter, and some jeering at that.

Five more goblins peeked their large heads out from behind bits of furniture or even tools.

They were almost all tiny goblins. Ordinary ones are three feet tall. These were all small, two feet or lesser. The obese one trapped under the Christmas lights was the largest.

Thomas pointed his gun at the trapped one.

“Surrender or the fat one gets shot.” He looked around. He didn’t truly expect it to work. Goblins loved violence, even if it was on one of their own. But he had his suspicions about these ones. This many concentrated in one place and with so little destruction.

The shouts continued. They did indeed love it.

“No! No! Please! I can’t surrender!” He screamed in that toad like voice.

*Can’t?”

“Explain.” He kept the gun trained on the tangled goblin.

“He can’t do that either, mister.” A female goblin, snarled at him with laughter. “None of us can.” Her smile widened showing her sharp teeth. She was a smaller one, a foot and a half maybe, tall, and extremely thin, muscles protruding out at odd angles, she walked on all four limbs, stalking towards him.

Shit.

The Christmas lights went out.

A gunshot rang out as he fired, his gun was still trained on the fat goblin. He dove for the still open side door. He flew through it, landing on his shoulder. He tucked and rolled, but slammed against the side fence.

The female goblin appeared in the door frame and pounced at him. Thomas was still reeling from the crash landing, and wasn’t able to properly defend himself, he just barely managed to get his forearm in the way of her gaping jaw. She latched on, snapping and ripping at his forearm. His suit jacket offered some protection, but not much. He felt her teeth rip into his flesh.

He grunted and shoved the pistol’s barrel between his forearm and her mouth, prying her off. He fired once the barrel was in her mouth. Bits of goblin sprayed the side of the garage wall.

Six shots left.

He pulled the goblins corpse off his arm, tossing it to the side and clambered up to his feet. The other goblins wouldn’t be far behind. He followed the now dark chain of lights to the battery. It was unplugged, as if the lights had been yanked hard enough to unplug them. He was grateful they didn’t break.

He took a few quick steps forward to the battery, plugging it back in. He yanked on the lights and they formed a barrier stopping the other goblins from exiting the garage.

A goblin wouldn’t have been able to unplug the battery. He was right, there was something else. A goblin queen shouldn’t have been able to either. He had no clue what he was up against now. For a practitioner, that was the worst case scenario.

Thomas stomped up the steps to his apartment. He was on the thirteenth floor. If he was superstitious he would’ve been perturbed. But he wasn’t. Every number was an unlucky number on at least one culture.

It was a large social housing block, filled with junkies and drug dealers. He’d gotten used to the lower floors being essentially a warzone for crackheads and gangbangers. The elevators were permanently down, so the upper floors were comparatively fine though. Only the desperate wanted to walk up twenty flights of stairs.

Not that he was that desperate it was just convenient.

He had finished his law degree and promptly got denied from all the major firms. They all cited “not enough experience” for entry level jobs and even unpaid internships.

Apparently even scholarships and being one of the best in an Ivy league college weren’t enough to secure a position these days.

He carried on though, found work at a local hardware store while he looked.

He was mostly numb to the violence in the area by now, he wasn’t particularly shocked by the news of murders being found on the upper floors.

Except for the pattern. 

It started with floor four. Four deaths each four days apart. 

Then floor seven. The same pattern: seven deaths, seven days apart. Police has dropped by at some point to ask questions, but nothing came of it.

It didn’t even appear in the news.

It wasn’t until it reached the 9th floor did he begin to worry. His normal route up the stairs took him past one of the victim’s apartments. Blood oozed from beneath the door. Dried brown and black. No one had bothered to clean it up.

He didn’t stick around to gawp. 

Soon enough, it was the thirteenth floor. 

He tried to take a vacation. Every thirteenth day he tried to be elsewhere. And yet every time, he ended up back at home. Hotels cancelling his bookings, his friends not responding to knocks on their doors. Hotels cancelling his bookings or not even having him registered on their reservations lists. One time an ice storm prevented him from just driving away.

He was smart enough to believe in luck by now. Something wanted him here. So he prepared.

He stole tools and materials from work to barricade his apartment. He bought a gun for the first time in his life, he even went to a shooting range to practise. A small but powerful pistol.

He sat in his small bedroom-office and waited. The night of the thirteenth victim on the thirteenth floor. His gun was in his hand.

There was knocking on the door. Not of his apartment, but on his office.

His stomach dropped and his heart raced.

Knock knock knock

He began counting instinctively. 

Thirteen knocks.

He stood up and walked to the door, quietly.

The knocking was coming from low down?

He cracked the door open, and saw a tiny hand wave jovially through the crack, at the height of his knees. The sleeve was bright green. 

And the hand was gone.

“Hello there, sir. Might we play a game?” He twirled around.

His eyes had a hard time looking at the thing. As if his eyes didn’t even want to acknowledge it.

It was a short man. An evil grin on his orange bearded face, sharp little teeth. 

A fucking leprechaun?!

“I see you’re well prepared. But probably not for me, hey?” He spoke with a jovial Irish accent. A mockery of one anyway.

The door drifted open behind Thomas. He let it.

“You see, all your friends didn’t believe in superstition either, and when I offered them to play my game or die a gruesome death, well most declined. The last few have been quite savvy though. Here’s hoping you’re a little better.”

“What are you?”

“Ooh where are me manners? I’m just a traveller, wanting to play a little game.”

“That’s not what I meant.” Thomas declared.

“What are you?” 

“Now now, don’t get snippy, good sir.” The little thing bared his teeth. “I just want to play a game.”

“You keep saying that. What game?”

“Now we’re talking. You’ve got quite the resume, ya know that? From all the others here.” The leprechaun pulled out a very long scroll, pretending to read it, pulling more and more paper from god knows where.

“A lawyer?” He sounded genuinely intrigued. “You stand a better chance than most then.” His smile widened.

“Tell me sir, would you ever lie in a court of law?” Another, somehow even wider grin.

“I-I would endeavour not to.” He answered, honestly. Something about the leprechaun made him wary. He’d read about them, of course, tricksters. This whole thing seemed like some trick.

“Interesting answer from one as innocent as yourself.” The leprechauns eyes narrowed. “Tell, me, why did you want to become a lawyer?”

“Is this the game you wanted to play? To ask me questions?” 

“Oooooh yes, yes it is, these questions are very important, you see. If you want my gold you’ll have to answer honestly.”

“I don’t want your gold. I just don’t want to end up like the others. Did you do that?”

“Now now, don’t be so hasty, and don’t go accusing Others so readily. You don’t know the rules yet and you’re already doing oh so well.”

“Tell me the rules then.” 

“If you answer at least one of my three of my next questions with Truth, I’ll give you my gold if not, I’ll set him on you.” The leprechaun smiled and gestured to an impossibly large half wolf half man, somehow standing next to him. His head was that of a wolf’s, his body, a naked man, impossibly muscular. His snout was long and grey, teeth covered in blood, dripping down onto the floor.

Thomas reeled backwards, dropping his gun.

How did I not noticed that?!

“You really aren’t aware. You lawyers are good.” 

Thomas reached for his gun, but it was gone. The leprechaun brandished it somehow.

“What is your name?” He said idly, looking at the gun absent-mindedly.

“Thomas.” 

“Your name is Just Thomas?” The leprechaun asked.

He didn’t answer.

The little man grunted. “If you don’t answer, there will be consequences.”

“No, not just Thomas. My name is Thomas Campbell.”

The leprechaun grinned. “Well done. Two more questions then.”

The gun was gone then, back in Thomas’s hand. He didn’t know what to do with it. It seemed foolish to shoot anything in his situation.

“What is your occupation?” 

“I work in a hardware store.”

“Interestiiiing.” The leprechaun smirked. “That was my third question. But you have answered none of them with Truth.”

What? I didn’t lie?

The leprechaun smiled, widely, and emitted a soft giggle.

“You lawyers are all the same, so easily tricked.” Then the leprechaun was gone. The wolf-man remained.

It bared it’s teeth and lunged forward jaw first, it snapped at him. He got a shot off first. The bullet went straight through the wolf-man’s body, a tiny spurt of blood shot out then was gone. No bullet hole, but he was distracted. 

Thomas fell backwards, through the now open door to the hall of his apartment.

A wolf man? Wait… A fucking were-wolf?!

The wolf-man lunged again, this time Thomas was ready. He dove forwards underneath the wolf-man. Straight for his desk, his small jewellery box.

He grabbed at his target and held it up as the wolf-man lunged again.

The silver chain wrapped around the wolf-man’s muzzle, and burned into it, creating a deep sizzling scar. The smell of burnt hair and flesh filled the room.

“Stop attacking me and I’ll pull this off you.” Thomas roared.

The wolf-man howled in rage and pain. “I accept your deal!” 

Thomas did so.

From then on Thomas was aware. His first Other he caught. A werewolf spirit. His preparation had done nothing this time, just pure luck. He vowed not to get caught out like that again.

He pulled what information he could from the wolf-man, who was surprisingly reasonable once he was bound. He gave Thomas details on how the world worked. He sought out other aware, learnt about the world and it’s Others. He learnt he could make a living from them, catching, working with them. His legal skills finally put to use, creating contracts with monsters.

He pulled his suit jacket off and tied it across his bleeding forearm. Superficial wound, but still. Meanwhile, he thought.

The battery was yanked rather than unplugged or just switched off. Whatever had done it, that was their only way of dealing with it. They couldn’t have gone outside, or use something to cut them either. They must have done it from inside…

Realisation hit him as well. It had been there with him then. Whatever it was, it was with him in the garage. And he didn’t see it.

Shit. Why are guns are never that useful?

It was something incorporeal. He looked mournfully at his pistol. Little threats like goblins were one thing.

But that lead him closer to a solution. He wouldn’t need weapons. Not physical ones anyway. It hadn’t spoken to him, but it must be capable of entering a contract. The goblins said they couldn’t surrender or explain.

If it could make a contract with goblins. Surely it could make one with him.

He straightened up, brushing his sandy blond hair back into order, he dropped the Christmas lights and strolled up the house.

He knocked on the front door.

It swung open.

The inside was ordinary enough. An entrance hallway leading into a large living room, complete with a fireplace, television, and several large, and rather plush looking sofas.

He was let in willingly this time. He’d been offered hospitality. There were certain rules in play now, regardless of his earlier actions. Whatever this Other was, it would have to follow them.

“Hello, thank you for granting me sanctuary in your residence. I wish to speak with you regarding your tenancy here.” He spoke, as courteously as possible.

A voice, female, echoed from the walls. “You have taken two of my children from me, practitioner. Now you expect me to talk?”

Thomas felt a cold hand brush his shoulder. He spun around, looking. But nothing was there. Well, that wasn’t it, it was there, he just couldn’t see it.

“Your children have been causing a mess here. They are bound, as are you, interfering with innocents is prohibited.”

“Practitioners, so quick to judge us Others.” The voice was like ice down his back, chilled, but it was happy. “Only unprovoked harm to innocents.”

“This woman and their family provoked you?” Thomas asked.

“The woman treats her children poorly. No mother could accept it. It is an afront to motherhood!” It screamed, the voice becoming momentarily tangible in the air.

Shit.

“That may be so, but you can’t hurt them.”

“We haven’t harmed the children!”

“What do you need to move on, revenant?” The name resonated through the air.

The air was silent.

A not-quite woman appeared in front of him. As if she was displayed on an old CRT television with poor signal. Static and noise as she blinked in and out. She was dressed in a old style nanny’s outfit. Black dress, white apron, the works.

Fucking Others. So stereotypical.

“I will not be leaving until that woman has learnt her lesson.” She snarled at him. She disappeared, her voice became disembodied once more, the lights flickering as she did so.

“A poor excuse. You’ve been around a long time, clearly. This isn’t the first mother you’ve hassled by the looks of it.” A poor excuse maybe, but it wasn’t a lie… “I can help the woman, she’s disturbed, she’d hardly make a good mother when going through this. Even though she can’t see you. Innocents know.”

“The woman needs to be trained!” The house recoiled at his suggestion.

“I could help move you to a happier family. Where the children are better taken care of.”

She didn’t dignify him with a response, just another scream. “Bring back the family! The woman must hurt!”

There goes the easy way… He couldn’t make a third peaceful offer safely.

“I ask you again: what do you need to move on?”

The revenant appeared in front of him, she lunged forward close and screamed directly in his face. His ear drums felt like they’d burst. She vanished again. This time, the lights went out.

He heard scampering around the room.

The goblins were back.

Thomas pulled out his gun, and he looked. The ordinarily thin lines connecting contracted beings was faded. Concentrated around the goblins but dissipating into thin air.

But enough to track the goblins. He watched the first hide behind a couch. He shot his pistol. A blood curdling scream came from the air as the goblin’s face was torn to shreds. A puddle of blood and gore appeared from around the corner of the couch.

Another goblin screamed, announcing its jump through the air. Thomas spun on his heel and pulled out his lighter, his implement. Poorly chosen, but still useful.

Click.

A whip of flame lashed out, striking the goblin in two.

Another scream from the revenant and the room shook violently. Thomas lost his footing.

The trees towered over him. The hike through the woods was long, his cigarette was long gone by now and Thomas was getting antsy.

This should be an open and shut case at least. This Other was a simple one. It hid and only killed you if you looked at it. The problem was that it it was bad at hiding or it was showing parts of itself to innocents on purpose as an excuse. Either way, he’d put a stop to it.

He was well off the trail now, far enough that he was lost. Intentionally, of course. 

It only showed itself when you were lost.

It started with a feeling. The hairs stood up on the back of Thomas’ neck. The eery feeling of being watched crawled up his back and burrowed into his heart. It’s pace quickened. Thomas himself was quite calm. He knew how Others worked. A purely physical fear reaction.

If he looked around now he was sure he’d see some subtle movement hiding behind a tree nearby, he’d look at it, then it would dart around, make him paranoid. On and on, so predictable.

It should be simple enough to stop, with a bit of trickery. It just needed to be seen fully, it would only move away if it was in the corner of your eye, so you could never quite catch it. The catch was that it was good enough at hiding that seeing it with your own eyes was almost impossible, and you had to be alone for it to find you.

The solution? Just ask it.

“Would you kindly show yourself?” Thomas asked the forest spirit. He looked around and saw something: a furry arm with long claws, it retreated behind a tree and was gone.

He didn’t chase it, or go searching. That’s one.

He felt the urge to light a cigarette, but he resisted. Just taking a deep breath instead. He kept his gaze straight once more. He kept walking, waiting for the feeling of being stalked to return.

Soon enough, it was there, his hair standing on end.

He sighed again, stopping and commanded. “Please show yourself.” He turned around and saw its leg, sickeningly thin with matted brown fur. It pulled it away and out of sight slower this time. 

That makes two.

He kept walking, his hand reached into his pocket and felt the small ball, still slightly wet and squishy. He was grateful he wasn’t squeamish.

When his heart began beating faster, knowing he was being watched, he clutched at the small ball, ready to toss it upwards. He kept walking though, he spotted a big enough tree ahead of him. He walked in front of it and placed a small plastic hula-hoop on the ground right by it. Then he kept walking.

He asked for the third and final time. “Show yourself.” He waited then he tossed the eyeball up. The still bloody eye spun around and locked onto the Other. The monster hadn’t had a chance to move even partially behind a tree.

Thomas turned around and saw it. Caught perfectly in the hula-hoop next to the tree, the glistening eyeball trained on it.

✦

Thomas landed on his hands, rolling gently onto his side, lessening the fall.

The house had darkened. It wasn’t pitch black yet, some sunlight was still coming from the windows, but it was fading fast. 

He lurched forward as another goblin dove at him, teeth bared wider than any mouth should ever open. The goblin overshot crashing into the wall next to Thomas, it’s teeth sunk into the plaster and it had trouble pulling them out. Thomas kicked out. The goblins teeth came free, both of the plaster and of the goblins own mouth. It went sailing towards the kitchen. Teeth disappeared into the carpeted floor.

Click 

A small firelight appeared in the centre of the room, covering the normally pleasant white room in a red and orange glow. 

Thomas stood and raised his pistol. He had five shots left. Hopefully there were fewer than five goblins left.

He looked around, searching for the scurrying green fuckers. But they were gone. The one he’d kicked into the kitchen had left a pool of blood, glistening orange in the fire light. But the goblin was nowhere to be seen.

The sunlight was gone now. He hadn’t noticed it. His firelight was a bad move.

Shit.

It was just darkening. The house was shifting. The windows weren’t just dark, they were gone. The walls he thought were red because of the fire, but no. They were red, they were wet. His hand left the wall glistening with a dark red substance.

Blood. 

Now he looked closer he could see the walls breathing. Deep ragged breaths. Anger. 

“Show yo-” the house tipped, only barely a foot. But his feet slipped on the now wet innards of the house’s flooring. He slid down the living room, away from the kitchen.

This is a strong fucking revenant. 

He shot towards the centre of the house. Not out of desperation, but he might as well. Three shots rang out and blood spurted from the walls.

The walls began vibrating and a piercing scream came from everywhere. But the floor righted itself.

Thomas gritted his teeth, and charged for the kitchen.

Where was that damned goblin?!

He saw it, cowering in the corner. He jumped at it. The house roared. The ceiling’s dangling light shot out and whipped Thomas away from the goblin. The glass bulb shattering glass over the floor, cutting Thomas’ shoulder. He persisted. Pretending to lunge for the goblin, he dove to the ground. He grabbed some glass and shoved it into the cowering goblins face cutting it and his hand in the process. 

He grabbed the goblin with his bloody hand.

The house screamed, the bloody walls vibrating. The house tipped fully on its side and Thomas grabbed the goblin, using it to soften his landing on the kitchen counter. 

That’s two. 

The goblin cried out, begging for it to stop.

“Show yourself!” Thomas commanded the revenant for the third time. It appeared before him and slashed out with claws.

The goblin shrieked as its face was clawed open.

The revenant stood in shock. Flickering in and out like static. It tried to fade out, re-join with the house but it was pulled back.

The house rightened itself, the wet walls become ordinary paint. The rotation undone.

“You’ve hurt those under your protection three times. You’ve shown yourself three times. Come with me and I will help you find peace. Or burn.” He offered a solution for the third time, holding his lighter out, ready to scorch the house.

The revenant looked at him, scowling, and knelt.
