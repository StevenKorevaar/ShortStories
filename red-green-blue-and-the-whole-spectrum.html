Red, Green, Blue, and the Whole Spectrum

Coming soon.

The colours on her screen flickered, back and forth between entertainment and news. The world was going to hell. But when was it not?

She couldn’t keep her mind focused on any one thing anymore. She flicked through feeds like someone from the long gone past would flick through channels on a television, nothing quite to their liking. But this was flicking through everything and anything. A boredom so profound there was nothing that could keep her attention.

She couldn’t even be entertained by her own frustration at her boredom. She searched, day in and day out for something that would occupy her mind.

When she couldn’t find anything she lay in her pod. Her eyes would flicker back and forth as if they were still watching something. 

Her screens kept playing, trying to find something to grab her attention. She twisted the screen away from her face and stared at the ceiling. Listening to the changing voices, music, and all other kinds of sounds and tunes that the feed could think of to make her look at it. 

Her arm dropped and she couldn’t gather the strength to lift it up to turn the sound down. 

Her ceiling was a mess. Pipes and wires woven across it. No one bothered to cover it all up.

The only thing that forced her attention were work notifications. An obnoxious pinging sound. It went off and her adrenaline spiked. Not enough to make her jump out of bed, but enough for her to lift her arm and move the screen back over her eye to read the notification. 

It was just a colleague sending her a meme. She felt one day she’d be called out for barely working. But then, who actually did any work these days? 

She did actually do some work on occasion, when there were actual deadlines. It was some of the only things in her life that could hold her attention. Out of necessity maybe, but still. Using her mind and body to problem solve basic things. But when she was done she was so tired. She couldn’t bring herself to try and do the same for her personal life.

This was her life. What little energy she had was spent on the little work that still had to be done by humans. The rest was spent giving in to the feed.

The second ping didn’t spike her adrenaline, she had none left. She flicked over to her notifications. A message from her manager.

All hands meeting for the data validation team, Thursday 2pm. Thank you!

Claire sighed and flicked back to her feed. At least she had today and Wednesday to rest. She probably didn’t need to do anything for the meeting.

The rest of the day went by in red, green, blue colours of the pixels on her screens, washing her eyes and mind clear of thoughts. 

✦

The train flew by her face, blowing her hair in front of her face. She moved through the neon lights as if she were blind. The overwhelming stimulation of lights and sounds blanking out her senses. She moved on instinct on her way to work, eyes and ears glazed over.

She only noticed when hair blew into her eyes, making them twitch, else she was dead to the world.

The people moved around her like water flowing through the rocks. Unnoticing, uncaring, unalive.

She worked at a tall multiple city block wide building covered in LED screens advertising all sorts of sensory enhancers, dopamine boosters, and neural doping. She did some sort of data cleaning and validation for a prosthetics company. Linking neural signals with low voltage control systems. It was all noise for her. Wavelengths and frequencies, matching one to another. It stimulated her, but she didn’t enjoy it. 

The elevator chimed as it arrived. A sludge of black and grey suits disembarked, parting around her and the other masses as they pushed their way on. Her floor was recognised and lit automatically. She still had to double check it.

She waited, barely noticed the shoulders jostling her to get on and off. Her floor chimed again, spoken in a voice that was barely recognisable as human. She wasn’t the only one to get off on her floor for a change. Another grey thing followed after her. She drifted into the centre of the building, flowing past more LED screens advertising their company’s own products and internal memos. None of it caught her attention. She tried to read them, coming into work forced her to pay attention, lest she be asked a question. Slowly she brought her mask to her face. Up from her feet, less dragging more stepping. Her legs moving confidently, each step placed with purpose. Her back straightening. Her head lifted. Her eyes piercing. She plastered a soft attentive look on her face.

She looked human now, but she felt like a screen. Just colours emitted at a certain frequency to evoke some artificial image. A mockery of the real thing.
