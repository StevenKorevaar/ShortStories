Red, Green, Blue, and the Whole Spectrum

Coming soon.

Please note that what follows is a draft, you may read it, but everything is subject to change.

✦

The colours on her screen flickered, back and forth between entertainment and news. The world was going to hell. But when was it not?

She couldn’t keep her mind focused on any one thing anymore. She flicked through feeds like someone from the long gone past would flick through channels on a television, nothing quite to their liking. But this was flicking through everything and anything. A boredom so profound there was nothing that could keep her attention.

She couldn’t even be entertained by her own frustration at her boredom. She searched, day in and day out for something that would occupy her mind.

When she couldn’t find anything she lay in her pod. Her eyes would flicker back and forth as if they were still watching something. 

Her screens kept playing, trying to find something to grab her attention. She twisted the screen away from her face and stared at the ceiling. Listening to the changing voices, music, and all other kinds of sounds and tunes that the feed could think of to make her look at it. 

Her arm dropped and she couldn’t gather the strength to lift it up to turn the sound down. 

Her ceiling was a mess. Pipes and wires woven across it. No one bothered to cover it all up.

The only thing that forced her attention were work notifications. An obnoxious pinging sound. It went off and her adrenaline spiked. Not enough to make her jump out of bed, but enough for her to lift her arm and move the screen back over her eye to read the notification. 

It was just a colleague sending her a meme. She felt one day she’d be called out for barely working. But then, who actually did any work these days? 

She did actually do some work on occasion, when there were actual deadlines. It was some of the only things in her life that could hold her attention. Out of necessity maybe, but still. Using her mind and body to problem solve basic things. But when she was done she was so tired. She couldn’t bring herself to try and do the same for her personal life.

This was her life. What little energy she had was spent on the little work that still had to be done by humans. The rest was spent giving in to the feed.

The second ping didn’t spike her adrenaline, she had none left. She flicked over to her notifications. A message from her manager.

All hands meeting for the data validation team, Thursday 2pm. Thank you!

Claire sighed and flicked back to her feed. At least she had today and Wednesday to rest. She probably didn’t need to do anything for the meeting.

The rest of the day went by in red, green, blue colours of the pixels on her screens, washing her eyes and mind clear of thoughts. 

✦

The train flew by her face, blowing her hair in front of her face. She moved through the neon lights as if she were blind. The overwhelming stimulation of lights and sounds blanking out her senses. She moved on instinct on her way to work, eyes and ears glazed over.

She only noticed when hair blew into her eyes, making them twitch, else she was dead to the world.

The people moved around her like water flowing through the rocks. Unnoticing, uncaring, unalive.

She worked at a tall multiple city block wide building covered in LED screens advertising all sorts of sensory enhancers, dopamine boosters, and neural doping. She did some sort of data cleaning and validation for a prosthetics company. Linking neural signals with low voltage control systems. It was all noise for her. Wavelengths and frequencies, matching one to another. It stimulated her, but she didn’t enjoy it. 

The elevator chimed as it arrived. A sludge of black and grey suits disembarked, parting around her and the other masses as they pushed their way on. Her floor was recognised and lit automatically. She still had to double check it.

She waited, barely noticed the shoulders jostling her to get on and off. Her floor chimed again, spoken in a voice that was barely recognisable as human. She wasn’t the only one to get off on her floor for a change. Another grey thing followed after her. She drifted into the centre of the building, flowing past more LED screens advertising their company’s own products and internal memos. None of it caught her attention. She tried to read them, coming into work forced her to pay attention, lest she be asked a question. Slowly she brought her mask to her face. Up from her feet, less dragging more stepping. Her legs moving confidently, each step placed with purpose. Her back straightening. Her head lifted. Her eyes focused, piercing. She plastered a soft attentive look on her face.

She looked human now, but she felt like a screen. Just colours emitted at certain frequencies to evoke some artificial image. A mockery of the real thing.

There was a woman behind her now, who took some quick hops to fall in step behind her. 

“Hi, data validation?” She said chirpily.

“Yes! Hi, I’m Claire! I don’t think we’ve met before? Are you new?” Claire offered the lady a smile, trying to mirror her brightness.

“Yes! I’m Xiqin. Today is my first day.” She puffed out her chest, proud. She took another quick step in front of Claire, and held out a hand. 

It was a prosthetic. One of theirs. Not entirely noticeable by eye as one, unless you knew what you were looking for.

Claire hesitated before gingerly taking the hand in hers. It was cold. The flesh soft, but structurally unyielding. 

She smiled anyway. “Nice to meet you.” Claire lied.

The meeting was an introduction for Xiqin to meet their team. Claire tried her best to make conversation, but mostly stood watching as they chatted amongst themselves, seemingly happy to talk about mundane things. Her smile wavered, and was happy to blend into the background, to not be noticed, to not exist. She made her away from the group, pulling her screen down over her eyes, pretending to read some important documents. But she wasn’t using it. Her feed was blocked here there was nothing she could do but watch until they were dismissed. Claire was relieved when they were. She left for her cubicle as soon as she could. Her eyes relaxing, her mind at ease as she found comfort in her solitude. 

Her work was boring supposedly, but it was enough to keep her mind occupied. Others complained it was repetitive, but she strived to find the patterns. The frequencies of signals that fit with one another. Constructive interference here, a standing wave there. They fit, forming a cohesive melody of electricity and neural signals. Xiqin’s prosthesis probably contained her work somewhere. But it was tiring.

The work day ended. Claire didn’t leave right away. She began noticing creatures walking past her cubicle. She sat at her desk staring into her screens. No content, just static. The colours flickering on and off. She felt the pressure rising. She waited for the creatures to pass. She couldn’t face joining them yet. She might have to speak to them.

The stream of bodies lessened and she felt it was safe to leave. To head home to safety.

She was wrong. 

She exited her cubicle, the world around her slowly beginning to blur once more as her mind clouded. The cubicle door was closed and the world moved around her bringing her to the elevator. There was a tapping somewhere. On her shoulder. She felt it after she got in the elevator.

She fought to push away the static in her mind. Turning her head to see her shoulder, and what was tapping it.

It was Xiqin’s finger. The small woman was smiling at her. She said something before Claire was ready to listen, the sound was lost to the static. She smiled and nodded at the girl. Unsure of what to do. The woman just smiled and laughed. Her attention moving elsewhere. Claire tried to think of something to say it ask. But her mind offered no such respite.

Her trip home was a blur. Wading through the masses of human creatures. Flowing along side them or fighting the current. Uncaring and unfeeling. Just another body.

✦

Claire fell into her bed. Her thoughts ruminating on her interactions with the very straight short black haired woman. Her smile was so earnest. Claire couldn’t understand it. She didn’t know what to do. She sank into the feed. 

The red, green, blue pixels of her screen flashed against her eyes. The impulses and waves of its sound pummelled her eardrums. She couldn’t even feel the bed around her body.

Overwhelming her senses with input. Burning out the feelings of inadequacy. Overpowering her thoughts of the world. Injecting so much noise into the signals of her brain that she no longer existed. 

She didn’t know when the feed ended and her dreams started. They were one and the same. A collage of imagery, of feelings. Incoherent coherences of colours. With so much information but so little meaning. 

✦

The morning brought pain. Her eyes filled with rainbow static, her ears bursting with every drop of water against the window. Threatening to crush her mind. She pulled her screen down and attempted to navigate to get messages, call in sick. She could barely read. It took her several attempts to get it done. 

Her migraine only lasted several hours. But the rest of the day, just like her life, went by in a haze, drifting in and out of cloudy static and painful clarity. She at least had the weekend to recover. 

She filled the weekend with the colours of her screens. She’d found a new series to watch.  But it too only lasted for a weekend. A fleeting moment in the long list of moments. She was enthralled, but when it was over she couldn’t recall a single thing. It was something that had caught her interest but she couldn’t remember why now. Just another vague feeling of engagement, a lack of boredom. 

Monday came by quickly. Another morning navigating the flashing lights and thunderous sounds of the city. The train sped past, blowing the rain over her. Her clothes clung to her body. It made her uncomfortable enough. But any sensation, good or bad, was at least something.

Her workplace towered over her and the other small human creatures. It reminded her of how small they all were.

She made her way inside, the automated doors and elevators greeting her with chimes that barely registered with her. The elevator packed with creatures, now taking the shape of humans as Claire pulled herself to normalcy. She focused her eyes and ears, her mind working in overdrive to pay attention, to sense the world around her.

As if on queue. There was a tap on her shoulder. The new woman again. Smiling up at her. Claire was ready this time, to be normal and hold a conversation.

“Hi! I’m sorry about last Thursday, I was just so tired you know. How are you? Good weekend?” Claire asked, trying to sound bright. She knew her cold eyes and wavering smile would betray her. But Xiqin didn’t seem to care.

“Hello, Claire! Where were you last Friday?” She said, smiling brightly, an unbridled enthusiasm.

“Just needed some rest.” Her smile faltered. She was saved by the chime of the elevator. She breathed as she exited, the people slowly flowing around her. She let them past, walking slowly. Alone again.

She forgot, of course, that Xiqin was in her department. The short woman followed her to their cubicles. Claire didn’t know what to say. They just walked side by side. Xiqin seemed content with that though, she was humming some tune.

“This is me.” Claire said, laughing nervously, as they arrived at her cube.

“Same!” Xiqin went a step forward to the workspace next to Claire’s. Apparently she’d been assigned the cubicle next to hers. “I figured you missed me on Thursday, you didn’t say hello until the elevator.”

Claire froze. She’d been so absorbed, dead to the world. She didn’t notice.

“I’m sorry, I just get very focused.”

“It’s fine! Are you okay?” She asked, looking at her inquisitively.

“Yes, of course.” Claire smiled and sat down.

Her screens flared to life, the outside world drowned out by the frequencies and colours. Her body melted away and she was lost in her work.
